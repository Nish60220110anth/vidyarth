American Express (often shortened to Amex) is a global financial services company best known for its charge and credit card products. Here’s a concise overview:

Founded & Headquarters

Established in 1850, American Express is headquartered in New York City and has grown into one of the world’s largest payment networks.

  • Core Businesses
    • Cards & Payments: Offers personal, small-business, and corporate cards—ranging from basic rewards cards up to premium products like The Platinum Card and The Centurion Card.
    • Merchant Services: Processes transactions for merchants who accept Amex cards.
    • Travel & Expense Management: Provides booking services, expense-tracking tools, and corporate travel management.
  • Distinctive Features
    • Charge vs. Credit: Many Amex products are “charge” cards—requiring full payment each month—while others function like traditional credit cards.
    • Membership Rewards: A flexible points program that lets cardholders redeem points for travel, statement credits, gift cards, or transfers to airline/hotel partners.
    • Premium Benefits: Higher-tier cards include lounge access, travel credits, concierge services, and elite hotel status.
  • Global Reach
    Amex has a presence in over 130 countries and serves both individual consumers and large corporations. While acceptance historically lagged behind Visa/Mastercard in some markets, Amex continues to expand its merchant network.
  • Financial Profile
    It regularly reports annual revenues in the tens of billions of dollars, with a business model that combines interest income (on revolving credit products), card fees, merchant discount fees, and other financial services.

Overall, American Express distinguishes itself through premium card benefits, a strong rewards ecosystem, and a long-standing reputation in travel and expense management.
